Summary

Ph.D. candidate in Biostatistics at the University of Southern California (expected December 2026), developing statistical and machine learning methods for high-dimensional biomedical data. My work centers on integrative multi-omics — building models that combine molecular, environmental, and phenotypic information into interpretable structure rather than black-box prediction.

At USC I develop LUCID, a model for integrative clustering, association inference, and risk prediction across multi-omics data with environmental exposomes; I rewrote its R package for a 120× speedup and extended it with new integration strategies, regularization, missing-data imputation, and visualization. I also developed Praxis-BGM, a semi-supervised, transfer-learning clustering approach using natural-gradient variational inference that folds in prior information such as pathways — published in Bioinformatics and extended to single-cell and spatial transcriptomics (Xenium) for label transfer across datasets. Alongside the methods work I consult for environmental health and epidemiology labs on PFAS and multi-omics mediation studies, and collaborate with AIMS@USC on single-cell spatial pipelines.

In summer 2026 I was a computational biology intern at Cellanome, where I built a deep-learning framework for modeling cell trajectories from longitudinal live-cell imaging and integrated it with paired transcriptomics — surfacing morphodynamic cell states that earlier analyses had missed.

Previously at NYU School of Global Public Health, where I built BiTr-Unet — a CNN + vision-transformer model for 3D medical image segmentation — and a voxel-level multiple-testing framework using fully connected hidden Markov random fields, enabling full-brain FDG-PET analysis across hundreds of thousands of voxels to detect Alzheimer's disease regions.
